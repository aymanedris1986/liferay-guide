Categories, tags & assets

The classification layer that makes dynamic collections, search facets and cross-type listings possible. Small feature, disproportionately large payoff.

The asset framework

Web Content articles, Documents, Blog posts, Object entries and more are all assets. Being an asset means a thing automatically gets: categories and tags, search indexing, related assets, view counts, ratings and comments, and eligibility for a collection.

This is why a single dynamic collection can list "everything tagged onboarding" across articles, PDFs and records at once.

Categories vs tags

CategoriesTags
StructureHierarchical, inside a vocabularyFlat, free-text
Who creates themAdmins, up frontAnyone, while authoring
GovernedYes — a fixed, translatable listNo — typos multiply
Can be requiredYes, per vocabularyNo
Use forDepartment, Document Type, Audience, RegionAd-hoc keywords, campaign labels
Practical rule

If a collection, a navigation structure, or a permission depends on it, it must be a category. Tags are for search relevance and nothing you build logic on.

Vocabularies

A vocabulary is a named set of categories, and it is where governance lives:

  • Scope — site-level, or instance-level (shared across every site).
  • Restrictions — limit a vocabulary to specific asset types, so "Department" only appears on the content it belongs on.
  • Required — authors cannot publish without choosing a category.
  • Translatable — category names have per-locale labels, so an Arabic visitor sees Arabic facets.
Vocabulary "Department" (instance scope, required on Policy content) ├── Human Resources ├── Finance │ ├── Accounts Payable │ └── Treasury └── Operations Vocabulary "Audience" ├── All staff └── Managers only

How it connects

  • Collections — the main consumer
  • Web Content — categorised on publish
  • Documents — categorised too
  • Objects — entries can be assets
  • Segments — audience rules
  • Initializer tokens — [$TAXONOMY_CATEGORY_ID:X$]

Design it before you create content

Retro-fitting a vocabulary onto 4,000 existing documents means someone categorises 4,000 documents. Decide the vocabularies at the start of a project, restrict them to the right asset types, and mark them required. This is a fifteen-minute decision that saves a week.

DoDon't
Few vocabularies, each with a clear question ("which department owns this?")One giant "Tags" vocabulary that means nothing
Instance scope for cross-site conceptsDuplicating "Department" per site and hoping the names match
Restrict to asset typesShowing every vocabulary on every form and training editors to ignore them
Translate category namesEnglish-only facets on a bilingual intranet

Traps

Site-scoped vocabularies do not cross sites

A collection on Site A cannot filter by a category defined on Site B. If content is shared, the vocabulary must be at instance scope — and moving it later means re-tagging.

Deleting a category silently un-filters content

Items lose the association and drop out of every dynamic collection that filtered on it. The pages still render, just with fewer items.

Tags are typo magnets

onboarding, on-boarding and Onboarding are three different tags. Anything load-bearing belongs in a vocabulary.
